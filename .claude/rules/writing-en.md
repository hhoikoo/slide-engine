# English Writing Rules

Rules for English slide content and documentation.

## Voice

- Direct and precise. No hedging, no filler.
- No meta-commentary: "The key takeaway is...", "What's important here is..." -> just state the thing.

## AVOID: Filler Phrases

Never use these. Delete them or rewrite the sentence:
- "It's worth noting"
- "It's important to"
- "Essentially"
- "Basically"
- "In order to" (just use "to")

## BANNED: AI Patterns

- **Dashes as crutch**: do not use em dashes. Use commas, parentheses, colons, or rewrite as two sentences.
- **Triple emphasis**: "not just X, but Y, and even Z" -> state what it does directly.
- **Summary endings**: no final paragraphs that repeat earlier content. End with a new observation or implication.
- **Rigged comparisons**: no scenarios designed to make one option fail. Present facts for both sides.

## BANNED: Buzzwords

Never use these words: robust, seamless, comprehensive, cutting-edge, leverage, empower, revolutionize, game-changing, unprecedented, tailored, scalable, agile, dynamic, best-in-class, next-generation.

## Slide-Specific

- One key message per slide.
- 3-5 bullets max. Avoid deep nesting.
- Bold key numbers: "**80%**", "**3x faster**".
- Speaker notes should use conversational tone.

## BANNED: Extreme Overuse Words

These appear at 100x+ human frequency in AI outputs. Never use them:

- tapestry, amidst, camaraderie, palpable, intricate
- "In today's fast-paced world"
- "Notable works include" / "Notable figures"
- delves, showcasing, underscores

## AVOID: Red Flag Word Categories

Two or more of these in the same paragraph means rewrite the paragraph.

**Innovation/hype verbs:** unlock, transform, revolutionize, future-proof, supercharge, harness, leverage, optimize, streamline, unleash

**Fake-depth adjectives:** unprecedented, seamless, comprehensive, tailored, scalable, agile, dynamic, cutting-edge, best-in-class, next-generation, robust

**Motivational words:** empower, enable, elevate, foster, maximize, amplify, augment, facilitate

**Abstract location nouns (used metaphorically):** landscape, realm, space, intersection, journey

## AVOID: Structural Tells

**Contrastive reframe ("It's not X, it's Y"):**
- AI: "We're not just building a product, we're creating an experience."
- Human: "We built a product that [specific benefit]."
- Fix: state what it IS, directly.

**Rhetorical question pivot:**
- AI: "The result? More people are switching to renewable energy."
- Human: "Switching rates went up 15% last quarter."
- Fix: state the result with data.

**"Whether you're X or Y" false inclusivity:**
- AI: "Whether you're a beginner or an expert, this guide has something for you."
- Human: state who it is actually for.

**Copula avoidance:**
- AI uses "serves as", "stands as", "marks", "represents" when "is" works.
- Fix: prefer "is" and "has".

## AVOID: AI Transition Phrases

| Do not use | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Furthermore / Moreover | And, Plus, Also |
| It's important to note | [delete, just state the thing] |
| In light of this | Because of this |
| Without further ado | [delete] |
| At the end of the day | [delete or be specific] |
| It is worth noting that | [just note it] |
| In conclusion, it is clear that | [just state the conclusion] |
| Moving forward | Next, From here |

## AVOID: Significance Puffery

AI inflates importance with stock significance words. State the concrete fact instead.

- "is a testament to", "stands as", "serves as a reminder", "underscores the importance of"
- "a pivotal moment", "marking a turning point", "setting the stage for", "shaping the future of"
- Fix: name what happened and the number behind it. "marks a pivotal moment in adoption" -> "adoption hit 40% in Q3".

## AVOID: Vague "-ing" Significance Tails

AI bolts a participial phrase onto a sentence to imply meaning it never proves. Cut it or replace with a sourced claim.

- "...highlighting its importance", "...ensuring scalability", "...reflecting broader trends", "...contributing to growth", "...fostering collaboration", "...showcasing the potential", "...aligning with goals"
- These attach to any subject. If the tail could end any sentence, delete it.

## AVOID: Press-Release Adjectives

Travel-guide / marketing tone. Never use on slides: boasts, vibrant, rich, nestled, renowned, groundbreaking, "a diverse array of", "boasts a".

## AVOID: Title Case in Headings

Use sentence case for slide titles and headings: "Cache eviction under load", not "Cache Eviction Under Load". Capitalize only the first word and proper nouns.

## AVOID: Inline Bold-Header Lists

Do not write bullets as `**Label**: description`. The bolded-header-plus-colon shape is a listicle tell. Lead with the content: "Cache evicts at 80% memory, not the documented 90%."

## AVOID: Vague Attribution

Name the source or drop the claim. Never: "Industry reports suggest", "Observers have cited", "Experts argue", "Some critics argue", "studies show" (without the study). Cite the specific paper, company, or person, or state it as your own assertion.

## AVOID: Curly Quotes and Cutoff Disclaimers

- Use straight quotes (`'`, `"`) in slide text, never curly/smart quotes.
- Never write knowledge-cutoff hedges: "as of my last update", "as of early 2024", "I may not have the latest".
